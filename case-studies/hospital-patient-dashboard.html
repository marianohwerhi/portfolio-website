Hospital patient dashboard

400 hospital patient records cleaned and modelled in Excel into an interactive dashboard tracking recovery, cost, and department performance.

Tools
Excel, pivot tables
Records
400 patients
Departments
7
Slicers
4

The business problem

Green Valley Medical Center handles large volumes of patient information daily: demographics, treatment details, hospital stay length, cost, and recovery outcomes, stored in raw tables that were difficult to interpret quickly. Without visualisation, administrators struggled to monitor department performance and treatment effectiveness or make fast, data-driven decisions.

The approach

The dataset contained 400 patient records across department, treatment type, doctor, age, gender, treatment cost, hospital stay, and recovery score, checked for duplicates, blanks, and missing values (none found). Two calculated fields were added that were not present in the raw data, age group and cost category, to make department and demographic comparisons meaningful rather than working from raw ages and cost figures directly. Pivot tables were then built to summarise recovery, cost, and stay length by department, doctor, treatment type, and gender, followed by an interactive dashboard with slicers for each.

Key findings

  • Recovery score varied by department, ranging from roughly 71 to 77 across the seven departments tracked.
  • Longer hospital stays were more often associated with more complex, higher-cost cases.
  • Patients receiving surgery or therapy showed marginally higher average recovery scores than those under observation alone.
  • Presenting the same data through an interactive dashboard, rather than a static table, made department-level and doctor-level patterns visible in a way the raw spreadsheet did not surface on its own.

Recommendations

  • Continue using dashboards, not raw tables, for ongoing performance monitoring.
  • Prioritise resource planning for departments showing higher treatment costs.
  • Use recovery trends by treatment type to inform care strategy discussions.
  • Longer term, connect the dashboard to a live hospital data source instead of a static export to support real-time reporting.

Where this project fits

This project predates the HIV analysis and demonstrates the Excel fundamentals that underpin the later Power BI work: pivot tables, calculated fields, and slicer-driven dashboard design. The source workbook and full project notes are on GitHub.
